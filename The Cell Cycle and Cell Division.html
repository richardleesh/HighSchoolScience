1. Introduction to Cell Division

Reference: Chapters 2.3–2.5, pages 36–45

Cells divide for THREE main reasons:

  1. Growth
  2. Repair
  3. Reproduction

3. Cell Division for Growth & Transport

Cellular Needs & Wastes

  • Cells need: Energy, nutrients, Water, and gases.
  • Many chemicals must be dissolved in water to be used by the cell.
  • Cells must excrete: Carbon Dioxide and wastes.

Movement across the Membrane

  • Diffusion: Movement of chemicals from an area of higher concentration to an area of lower concentration.
  • Osmosis: Movement of water down a concentration gradient. More precisely, osmosis is water moving across a selectively permeable membrane down its water potential gradient.

Why Cells Can't Grow Infinitely Large

  • Question: What happens when a cell gets too LARGE?
    • Answer: Materials such as chemicals and water cannot move into, through, and out of the cell fast enough to meet its needs.

4. The Stages of the Cell Cycle

The three main stages of the cell cycle are:

  1. Interphase
  2. Mitosis
  3. Cytokinesis

5. Stages of Interphase & Mitosis Overview

Interphase (Slide 12)

  • Interphase is the longest stage for most cells.
  • During Interphase, the cell carries out ALL life activities EXCEPT cell division.
  • DNA is in very long, thin, invisible strands called chromatin.
  • Two key events in Interphase:
    1. DNA is duplicated (replicated) so there are 2 identical strands of genetic material.
    2. More organelles are formed, and spindle fibers begin to form.

Mitosis & Cytokinesis Overview (Slide 13)

  • Mitosis is the division of the nucleus.
  • Mitosis consists of 4 stages (abbreviated PMAT):
    • P: Prophase
    • M: Metaphase
    • A: Anaphase
    • T: Telophase
  • Cytokinesis is the division of the rest of the cytoplasm and cell membrane.
  • Each complete cell division produces two genetically identical daughter cells.

6. The 4 Stages of Mitosis

Stage 1: Prophase

  • DNA condenses into a compact form called chromosomes.
  • A chromosome has 2 identical strands called chromatids held together by a centromere.
  • The nuclear membrane disappears during prophase.

Stage 2: Metaphase

  • Spindle fibers PULL the chromosomes into the equator (middle) of the cell.
  • Centrioles / centrosomes produce the spindle fibers that separate the chromosomes.

Stage 3: Anaphase

  • The centromere splits, and sister chromatids separate / pull apart.
  • They are now called daughter chromosomes.
  • Daughter chromosomes move to opposite sides of the cell.

Stage 4: Telophase

  • Daughter chromosomes stretch, thin out, and are no longer visible.
  • A new nuclear membrane forms around each group of daughter chromosomes.
  • The cell appears to have two nuclei.

7. Cytokinesis

During cytokinesis, the cytoplasm divides, producing 2 identical daughter cells.

Compare cytokinesis in plant and animal cells:

Cell TypeCytokinesis Process
Plant CellsA cell plate develops between daughter cells into a new cell wall.
Animal CellsThe cell membrane is pinched off in the center.

8. Checkpoints in the Cell Cycle

At specific checkpoints, proteins monitor cell activities and surroundings. These proteins send signals to the nucleus regarding whether cell division should occur.

List the 4 reasons a cell will remain in Interphase (NOT divide):

  1. Signals from surrounding cells indicate that no more cells are needed
  2. There are not enough nutrients for cell growth
  3. The DNA within the nucleus has not been copied
  4. The DNA is damaged
